What are waves?

In physics, a wave is a disturbance that propagates through space or a medium, transferring energy from one place to another.

Types of waves

→ Electromagnetic waves:

They are transmissions of energy through space, whether it's empty or not. Meaning, they do not need matter to travel. Their speed in vacuum is approximately 299,792,458 m/s, which is the speed of light. (or in scientific notation, 3 x 108).

→ Mechanical waves:

They are transmissions of energy through material media, this means they cannot travel through vacuum. Their speed depends on: The material, The temperature, and Molar mass. In particular, for gases, the speed of sound is given by:

Gamma = adiabatic constant of the gas
R = universal gas constant
T = temperature
M = molar mass
This means that sound travels faster in hotter gases or lighter molecules(less molar mass).

→Gravitational waves:

They are transmissions of energy through space-time. Like ripples that carry that energy across the cosmos, produced by accelerating massive objects, such as merging black holes or neutron stars.

Fundamental concepts

  • Frequency
  • Period
  • Wavelength
  • Speed of a wave

What is frequency?

→ Frequency is the measure of how many times a phenomenon repeats itself per unit of time. In wave phenomena such as sound, electromagnetic waves, electrical signals, and others, frequency expresses the number of cycles the wave repeats per second.It's measured in hertz (Hz)

What is Period?

→ It is the time it takes for one complete oscillation to occur - the time it takes for the motion to repeat itself.

What is Wavelength?

→ It is the length of one complete wave cycle, and its value is the distance between two equivalent points on the wave. In physics, the wavelength is defined as the distance traveled by a periodic disturbance through a medium during one cycle.

What is the speed of a wave?

→ Wave speed is the rate at which a wave disturbance travels through a medium or space. It depends on the properties of the medium and the type of wave.
